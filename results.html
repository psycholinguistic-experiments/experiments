    Translation priming, one dot per person

    Reaction times

    Correct responses to real words only, 200–5,000 ms, as in Chaouch-Orozco et al. (2021, 2023). Means of each person's own mean. Test: each person's priming effect against 0 (a one-sample t-test, the same as a paired t-test of translation vs unrelated); dz = mean effect ÷ its SD. Two-tailed, α = .05.

    Accuracy

    Every word trial the student saw properly, right or wrong, with no reaction-time trimming. Priming = errors after unrelated − errors after translation, in percentage points; positive = more errors without the translation. Same test as for reaction times. Non-words are shown as a check that students were doing the task.

        English proficiency and the effect

        Each dot is a student in the English version. Black line: regression on English self-rating, with its 95% band. Dashed line: the Chinese group's mean. If the effect fades as English improves, the line moves toward the dashed line at higher ratings.

        Slope = change in the score per 1-point rise in the self-rating, tested against 0. Interaction = English slope − Chinese slope, with a 95% CI and its test; a significant interaction means proficiency changes the effect. In the Chinese version, English proficiency should not matter.

        All measures

        Means of each student's own score. Difference = English − Chinese, with a 95% confidence interval. Welch's t-test (unequal variances), two-tailed, α = .05; d = difference ÷ pooled SD. Only students with Chinese as a first language who used no translation aid.

        English self-ratings

        1 = very limited, 7 = native-like. The two groups should be similar (Welch's t-test).
