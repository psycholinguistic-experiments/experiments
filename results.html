Summary

    Translation priming, one dot per person

    Reaction times

    Correct responses to real words only, 200–5,000 ms, as in Chaouch-Orozco et al. (2021, 2023). Means of each person's own mean. Test: each person's priming effect against 0 (a one-sample t-test, the same as a paired t-test of translation vs unrelated); dz = mean effect ÷ its SD. Two-tailed, α = .05.

    Accuracy

    Every word trial the student saw properly, right or wrong, with no reaction-time trimming. Priming = errors after unrelated − errors after translation, in percentage points; positive = more errors without the translation. Same test as for reaction times. Non-words are shown as a check that students were doing the task.

                Data quality

                Answers scored: found in the norms (Warriner et al., 2013; Chan & Tse, 2024). Valid transitions: adjacent answers that were both scored, out of 90 per student and language (answers 1→2 to 9→10 in 10 chains). An unscored answer breaks both of its transitions; nothing is bridged across it.

                Chinese valence is on the English scale: the Chan & Tse ratings are compressed towards the middle, so they are equated to Warriner over the two whole norm sets (English = −1.53 + 1.31 × Chinese; assets/data/equating.json). The downloads keep the raw ratings too.
